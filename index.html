03

System Deep-Dive

The four engineering systems that govern the conversion — what changes, why it matters, and how it is controlled.

🧽 1 · Cleaning to a distillate standard

The dominant challenge. Every trace of VLSFO clingage and sludge — especially in the dead spots around the retained heating coils and structure — must be removed so the tank meets LSMGO specification, not merely gas-free.

Mucking outHot alkaline washContinuous slop stripDistillate-clean acceptance

Acceptance: visual cleanliness, no oily film, no free residue around coils and structure — issued to the contractor in writing before work starts and signed off with a photo record.

🔧 2 · Steam coils — isolate & blank

Coils are retained, not removed. The steam supply/return is isolated at the manifold (without interrupting other tanks on the same main), and the penetrations are blanked from inside the tank.

Coil statusRetained in place
PenetrationsInternally blanked
RecordAs-fitted photos

Note Any future work on a blanked coil is a fresh enclosed-space entry and must remain traceable.

📟 3 · Level alarm & gauging

The density drop from HFO 0.960 to LSMGO ~0.86 (≈10%) directly affects pressure-based gauging. Re-calibration is mandatory before the tank enters service.

Pressure / hydrostatic typeRe-range — or reads ~10% low
Radar / float typeReading OK — verify setpoint
Sounding tablesUnchanged (geometry)
Loading computerUpdate density 0.96 → ~0.86

SOLAS High-level / overfill alarm function-tested at the corrected setpoint before service.

🌀 4 · Overflow / common vent

No hardware modification. There is no non-return device on the gravity overflow line, so cross-contamination is controlled operationally by ullage management.

WT4 S working levelBelow overflow at all times
Adjacent HFO tanksNot filled above overflow
Header (DL0403)Free-draining to overflow tank

Control Ullage management is recorded as a formal operational control — operational by design.
