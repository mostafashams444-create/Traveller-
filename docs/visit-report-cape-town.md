## 2. Getting there

**Flights.** There is normally no non-stop scheduled service on Lagos → Cape Town.
The usual pattern is a one-stop connection, most commonly via Johannesburg
(LOS → JNB → CPT), with Addis Ababa, Nairobi and Doha as alternative hubs.
Total door-to-door time typically runs 12–16 hours depending on the connection.

> Verify the routing and carrier at booking — schedules on this pairing change

## 3. Airport → city centre access

Cape Town International (CPT) sits roughly 20 km south-east of the CBD, linked
by the N2 highway. Typical transfer time is 25–40 minutes off-peak, longer in
the morning and late-afternoon peaks.

| Option | Roughly | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MyCiTi bus (airport route) | Cheapest | Runs airport ↔ Civic Centre station in the CBD. Requires a myconnect card, buyable at the airport kiosk. Fixed schedule, no late-night cover. |
| E-hailing (Uber / Bolt) | Mid | Designated pickup zone at the airport, signposted. Fare varies with surge. |
| Metered taxi | Higher | Ranked outside arrivals. Agree the fare or confirm the meter before departing. |
| Hotel / pre-booked shuttle | Higher | Worth it for a late-night arrival or first visit. |
| Car hire | Varies | Desks in the terminal. Left-hand-side driving. Consider whether you need a car at all if you are staying in the CBD. |

## 4. Moving around the city centre

The CBD is compact and largely walkable in daylight — the grid between the
Foreshore, Long Street and the Company's Garden covers most business and
visitor destinations on foot.

- **MyCiTi** covers the CBD, Waterfront, Sea Point and Atlantic seaboard on the
  same card used from the airport.
- **E-hailing** is widely used and generally the default for evening movement.
- **Walking** is fine by day on the main routes. After dark, prefer a car for
  anything beyond a short, busy stretch.
- **Minibus taxis** are cheap and heavily used by residents, but routes are not
  signposted for visitors — not a good first-visit option.

## 5. Access and practical notes

| Topic | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Currency | South African rand (ZAR). Cards are accepted almost everywhere in the CBD; carry some cash for parking attendants and small vendors. |
| Power | 230 V. South Africa uses its own large round-pin plug (Type M) alongside Type C/N — bring an adapter, hotel loaners run out. |
| SIM / data | Local SIM available at the airport; registration (RICA) requires passport and an address for your stay. |
| Load-shedding | Scheduled power cuts still occur. Check the current stage and your area's schedule; most hotels have backup, smaller venues may not. |
| Weather | Southern-hemisphere seasons — reverse of Lagos. Winter (Jun–Aug) is the wet, windy season; summer (Dec–Feb) is dry and very windy. |
| Safety | Normal city precautions: no visible valuables, no phone out at intersections, arranged transport after dark. |
| Emergency | 112 from a mobile; 10111 police; 10177 ambulance. |
